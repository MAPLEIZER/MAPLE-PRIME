# Chapter 1 — Constitutional foundations

Kenyan data-rights analysis begins with the Constitution. Article 31 protects privacy, including protection against unnecessary revelation of private/family information and infringement of private communications. The Data Protection Act gives effect to Article 31(c) and (d) in the personal-data context.

## KDR teaching rule

A surprising SMS, unknown lender contact or data mismatch can be a **signal to investigate**. It is not automatically proof that Article 31, the Data Protection Act or a criminal law has been violated.

## Questions to ask

- What information about the person was used or revealed?
- Was the communication intended for that person?
- Who determined why and how personal data would be processed?
- What lawful basis, notice or consent is claimed?
- Was the information shared with a third party?
- Is there evidence beyond the message itself?

Authoritative source: Kenya Law, Constitution of Kenya, Article 31.
